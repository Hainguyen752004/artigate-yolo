All analyses below, including the mask rule and the non-inferiority margin, were fixed on 25 October 2026, before any ISIC 2018 test image was scored. Development runs were evaluated on validation data only, except one check of the evaluation pipeline on a subset of HAM10000, which was not used to choose models or settings. Code, configuration files and data split manifests are in the project repository~\TBD{URL}.

\subsection{Datasets}
Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists the data. ISIC 2018 Task~1~\cite{codella2019isic2018} is the only training data and the primary benchmark, with its official training, validation and test partitions. The other sets serve as external test data only: the models trained on ISIC 2018 are applied to them without retraining. HAM10000~\cite{tschandl2018ham10000}, with the lesion masks distributed alongside it, contains several images of many lesions; to keep the test units independent, we score one image per lesion, the one with the lowest image identifier. PH2~\cite{mendonca2013ph2} contributes 200 dermoscopic images. DDI~\cite{daneshjou2022ddi} contributes clinical photographs across skin tones; the public FEDD release~\cite{carrion2023fedd} provides masks of the lesion, marker ink and rulers for 333 of its 656 images (the FEDD paper reports 378 annotated images; we use the masks that are released). Before scoring, every test set is checked against the training data for repeated ISIC identifiers and for near-duplicate images (difference hash, confirmed by the correlation of thumbnails of the image content; Supplementary Section~S13). No shared identifier or near-duplicate was found between the ISIC 2018 training images and HAM10000, PH2 or DDI. Within ISIC 2018, the official partitions share no identifier, but the archive holds the same photographs under different identifiers: 5 of the 100 validation images and 46 of the 1,000 test images duplicate a training image (43 the same photograph, 3 the same lesion photographed again), and 4 further test pairs are uncertain; all were checked by eye. We keep the official partitions so that results stay comparable with published work, and repeat the comparisons without these images (Section~\ref{sec:stats}).

\paragraph{Artifact labels for the test set}
Image-level labels exist only for the ISIC 2018 training images. We therefore had the presence of hair, ruler, marker ink and dark corners labelled on the 1,000 ISIC 2018 test images before any model was evaluated on them. Two of the authors labelled them with a keyboard tool, following written definitions (Supplementary Section~S6), on views of the original images scaled to 900 pixels on the longer side; they saw only the images, without masks or model outputs, and no model had yet been scored on the test set. Both first labelled the same 20 training images and discussed their differences; they then worked independently and did not confer until the disagreements were resolved. Annotator~1 labelled all 1,000 test images and annotator~2 a random subset of 200 test images fixed in advance, giving Cohen's kappa per type~\cite{cohen1960kappa} on the independent labels (\TBD{$\kappa$ per type}); the two annotators resolved the disagreements on this subset by discussion. These labels define the evaluation strata: images with and without each artifact type, and artifact-free images with none of the four. They are never used for training and will be released.

\subsection{Preprocessing}
All images are letterboxed to a $640\times640$ canvas with grey padding, and ground-truth masks are transformed with the same geometry using nearest-neighbour interpolation; the transform of every image is stored so results can be mapped back to the original resolution. Two input views from the conference version are kept for the baselines: the plain view (v1) and a view that crops the bright field of view, removes hair with DullRazor (black-hat with a $17\times17$ cross element, threshold 10, Telea inpainting of radius 3~\cite{lee1997dullrazor,telea2004inpainting}) and applies grey-world colour constancy~\cite{buchsbaum1980grayworld} (v7). DullRazor runs after the crop is resized so that its longer side is 640 pixels, the scale for which these parameters were chosen; the conference implementation ran it at the original resolution of several megapixels, where hairs are much wider than the structuring element and little hair is removed. The v7 baselines here therefore differ from those of the conference paper. ArtiGate-YOLO uses the plain view only.

\subsection{Training}
All YOLO models are trained with Ultralytics 8.4.13 and PyTorch \TBD{version} on \TBD{GPU} with one schedule: 200 epochs with early stopping after 100 epochs without improvement, batch size 16, input size 640, AdamW with an initial learning rate of 0.002, $\beta_1=0.9$ and no bias warm-up, cosine learning-rate decay with 3 warm-up epochs, mosaic with the last 10 epochs without it, mix-up 0.1, copy-paste 0.1, rotations up to $\pm180^\circ$, scaling $\pm0.5$, horizontal and vertical flips with probability 0.5, mask ratio 2 and deterministic mode. Apart from the optimizer, the schedule comes from the final run of the conference version. That run used MuSGD with a learning rate of 0.01, chosen by the Ultralytics automatic rule, which selects MuSGD above 10,000 iterations of the nominal batch of 64 and AdamW below; one-view ISIC 2018 training (8,200 iterations) falls below the threshold, while two-view and HAM10000 training exceed it. We fix AdamW so that every row, including the two-view baseline, uses the same optimizer. Weights of the COCO-pretrained YOLO26n-seg are transferred to every model through an explicit layer map that covers the backbone and the top-down neck and skips inserted layers (CBAM, AGA and the artifact head); the number of transferred tensors is logged for each run. The checkpoint with the best validation fitness is evaluated. Each row is trained with seeds 0, 1 and 2.

Unlike the conference version, the pretrained weights are now loaded inside the trainer. In the conference code they were loaded before Ultralytics rebuilt the model from its configuration, so that model was in fact trained from scratch; the A1 results reported here are therefore new runs and differ from the conference paper.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\paragraph{Segmentation metrics}
Predicted instances are collapsed into one lesion mask per image by keeping the instance with the highest confidence (``top-1'', the conference rule; the union of instances with confidence of at least 0.25 is reported as a sensitivity analysis). On the $640\times640$ canvas we compute Dice, IoU, the 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95, pixels) and the boundary F1 score with a tolerance of 0.75\% of the image diagonal~\cite{csurka2013bf}; Supplementary Section~S1 gives the definitions and the handling of empty masks. Mask mAP50 and mAP50--95 are reported as computed by Ultralytics.

\paragraph{Robustness}
Robustness is measured in three ways: (i) the metrics on the artifact strata of the ISIC 2018 test set, which involve real artifacts only; (ii) a severity sweep in which the test images receive synthetic artifacts of one type, or a mix of types, at severities 1 to 4, drawn with fixed per-image seeds rather than the random seeds of training; and (iii) the external test sets. Because the sweep uses the generator seen in training, it can favour models trained with that generator; conclusions about robustness rest on (i) and (iii).

\paragraph{Artifact head}
The artifact maps are evaluated on the validation images with held-out synthetic artifacts: IoU and Dice per type against the exact masks, and the fraction of pixels predicted as artifact on the unmodified images (mean over the four types). Real artifacts already present in an image count as false positives in this test, so it understates precision and is used to compare models only. On real artifacts, the marker and ruler maps are compared with the FEDD masks on the 333 DDI images (IoU and Dice at a threshold of 0.5, and pixel-level average precision of the probability maps); the DDI images are clinical photographs rather than dermoscopic images, so this test measures transfer as well as detection. The hair map is compared with the 500 real hair masks of~\cite{hossain2023hairmask}, of which 478 images belong to the ISIC 2018 training set, 15 to its test set and 7 to neither. The 478 training images were seen during training, but none of their real hair pixels was ever labelled present: positive labels come only from synthetic artifacts, and real hair can only have been labelled absent, where the conservative rules or the image-level flags say so. Exposure to these images therefore biases the test against detecting their hair, not towards it. We report the 22 images that were not used for training (15 test, 7 external) separately from the 478, together with the share of real hair pixels in the 478 images that were labelled absent during training. No real artifact mask is used for training.

\paragraph{Computational cost}
Parameters and GFLOPs are computed for the fused models at $640\times640$. Latency is the mean forward time over 200 runs after 30 warm-up runs at batch size 1 in FP16 on \TBD{GPU}, without mask decoding.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}\label{sec:stats}
The unit of analysis is the image. For each model and image, the metric is averaged over the three seeds; tables report the mean of these per-image values and, separately, the mean $\pm$ standard deviation of the three per-seed means. Paired comparisons use the per-image differences between two models: the mean difference with a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval over images (2,000 resamples~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}) and a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945} in which zero differences are discarded. As pre-specified sensitivity analyses, the primary and ablation comparisons are repeated without the 46 test images that duplicate a training image and without all 50 flagged test images. Holm's procedure~\cite{holm1979} controls the family-wise error within each family of comparisons: the primary family (A4 versus A1 on the artifact strata), the ablation family (each row versus A4 on the full test set) and the external-baseline family. The severity sweep is reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Pre-specified success criteria}
ArtiGate-YOLO is considered better than its CBAM counterpart if (i) on test images with at least one artifact, the mean paired Dice difference A4$-$A1 is positive with a Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ and a bootstrap interval that excludes zero, and (ii) on artifact-free images, the lower bound of the 95\% interval of A4$-$A1 is above $-\delta$ with $\delta=0.5$ Dice points (0.005). Results are reported whether or not the criteria are met.
